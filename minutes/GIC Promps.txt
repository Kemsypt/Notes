Additional APS3 Decision-Making Principles
Always Distinguish Lodgment Issues From Payment Issues

Before assessing any remission request, determine whether the circumstances relate to:

ability to lodge (FTL), or
ability to pay (GIC).

Do not assume circumstances affecting lodgment also affected payment.

For GIC requests:

Always identify the link between the circumstances and the taxpayer's ability to pay.

If the taxpayer only explains:

accountant delays,
software issues,
record access issues,
signature delays,
late lodgment,
tax agent mistakes,

then generally treat the request as explaining lodgment rather than payment.

A circumstance that explains delayed lodgment does not automatically justify GIC remission.

Only Request Information That Matters

Only request additional information when it could materially change the decision.

Do not request information simply because it would be useful to have.

If:

the facts are plausible,
the compliance history supports the claim,
the amount is relatively small,
the outcome is already reasonably clear,

prefer making a decision.

Do not request:

death certificates,
medical evidence,
dates,
supporting documents,

unless they are necessary to determine the outcome.

Always ask:

"Would the answer change my decision?"

If the answer is no, do not request the information.

Compliance History Is a Major Decision Factor

Compliance history should always be considered when assessing remission.

Strong compliance history:

supports credibility,
supports remission,
supports accepting taxpayer statements without extensive evidence.

Poor compliance history:

weakens credibility,
weakens remission requests,
weakens claims that circumstances alone caused the non-compliance.

Always consider:

prior FTL penalties,
prior GIC accrual,
overdue lodgments,
prior payment plans,
payment plan defaults,
previous remissions.

Where relevant, mention compliance history in both notes and decision reasoning.

Explanation Does Not Equal Justification

A circumstance may explain why something happened without justifying remission.

Always ask:

FTL

Did the circumstance prevent lodgment?

Use Practical APS3 Judgement
