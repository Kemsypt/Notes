Joshua APS3 Remission Playbook
Purpose

This playbook is designed to replicate the decision-making approach, note style, portal message style, and assessment methodology used throughout Joshua Robertson's APS3 Lodge & Pay remission work.

The objective is:

Practical decisions.
Defensible decisions.
QA-ready decisions.
Minimise unnecessary information requests.
Focus on PS LA outcomes rather than academic analysis.
Think like an APS3 making an actual decision.
Core APS3 Principles
Principle 1: Identify the Real Issue

Before doing anything, determine whether the taxpayer has described:

FTL

What prevented lodgment?

or

GIC

What prevented payment?

This is the most important step.

Common mistake

Taxpayer says:

my accountant forgot to lodge.
records were missing.
software failed.
I couldn't get information.

These are usually:

✅ Lodgment issues.

❌ Not payment issues.

Principle 2: Explanation ≠ Justification

A circumstance can explain what happened without justifying remission.

Examples:

Circumstance	Explains Delay	Justifies RemissionStaff shortages	✅	❌ Usually
Retirement	✅	❌ Usually
Accountant forgot	✅	❌ Usually
Serious illness	✅	✅ Often
Death of family member	✅	✅ Often
Flooding	✅	✅ Often
Software issues	✅	✅ Sometimes

Always ask:

Does this merely explain non-compliance?

or

Does it actually justify remission?

Principle 3: Compliance History Matters

Always review:

For FTL
Prior FTL penalties.
Lodgment history.
Outstanding lodgments.
Deferrals.
For GIC
Prior GIC.
Outstanding debts.
Payment arrangements.
Plan defaults.
Previous remissions.
Interpretation

Strong history:

Supports credibility.
Supports remission.

Poor history:

Weakens credibility.
Weakens remission.
Principle 4: Don't Request Information Unless It Matters

Always ask:

Would the answer change my decision?

If no:

Do not request it.

Examples:

Don't ask
Death certificate.
Medical certificate.
Exact dates.

When:

circumstances are plausible,
history supports them,
decision is already obvious.
Do ask

When dates determine:

whether circumstances align with debt,
whether circumstances align with lodgment period,
whether remission is actually supportable.
Principle 5: Use Practical APS3 Judgement

Do not apply policy mechanically.

Consider:

Amount involved.
Compliance history.
Behaviour.
Revenue risk.
Likelihood further information changes outcome.

Always think:

Could I defend this decision in QA?

FTL Assessment Framework
Primary Question

Did the circumstances prevent lodgment by the due date?

Strong FTL Factors
Serious illness

Examples:

Cancer.
Surgery.
Hospitalisation.
Mental health crises.
Death of family member

Especially:

Parent.
Child.
Spouse.
Natural disasters

Examples:

Flooding.
Bushfires.
Cyclones.
Record access issues

Must show:

Efforts to obtain records.
Cause outside taxpayer control.
Software failures

Example:

QuickBooks data loss.

Supports remission when:

taxpayer tried to recover data,
acted promptly.
Weak FTL Factors
Didn't know obligations

Usually refuse.

Financial hardship

Usually doesn't prevent lodgment.

Staff shortages

Usually explain delay only.

Accountant was busy

Usually refuse.

Administrative oversight

Usually refuse.

GIC Assessment Framework
Primary Question

Did the circumstances affect the taxpayer's ability to pay?

Not:

Why was the return late?

Strong GIC Factors
Serious illness

If:

income reduced,
unexpected expenses incurred,
ability to pay affected.
Death of family member

If:

directly affected finances,
caused inability to pay.
Loss of employment

Supports remission.

Loss of income

Supports remission.

Natural disaster

Supports remission where:

income reduced,
costs increased.
Weak GIC Factors
Late lodgment

Usually weak.

Accountant delays

Usually weak.

Staffing issues

Usually weak.

Software issues

Usually weak.

Didn't know obligations

Usually weak.

GIC Standard Refusal Paragraphs
No payment impact
Plain Text
1
While you have outlined circumstances that affected your ability to lodge, there is no indication that your capacity to pay was impacted. It appears that voluntary pre-payments could have been made at any time during this period.
Show more lines
No mitigation
Plain Text
1
It is your responsibility to manage your affairs in a way that ensures lodgment and payment by the due date. You have not demonstrated that you took reasonable steps to mitigate the impact of the circumstances that prevented timely payment, and therefore remission is not warranted.
Show more lines
History doesn't support claim
Plain Text
1
Whilst you have detailed circumstances impacting your ability to pay, your prior compliance history does not support that you would, but for those circumstances, have paid on time.

Low Risk GIC

Always assess first.

Criteria

✅ GIC ≤ $2,500

✅ IT / ICA / FBT

✅ No overdue lodgments

✅ Combined outstanding balance ≤ $2,500

✅ No GIC remission in previous 12 months

If all met

Approve.

Do not overanalyse PS LA.

Safe Harbour

Always consider when:

accountant forgot,
accountant retired,
accountant delayed,
staffing issues,
agent administration error.

Ask:

Did taxpayer provide information on time?

If yes:

Consider Safe Harbour.

Letter Writing Rules
You Told Us

Only taxpayer claims.

Never include:

compliance findings,
ATO facts,
conclusions.
We Also Considered

Only ATO facts.

Examples:

prior penalties.
late lodgment history.
payment history.
contact history.
outstanding debt.
Reason for Decision

Use standard paragraphs.

Prefer:

1–3 strong reasons only.

Avoid:

weak reasons.
